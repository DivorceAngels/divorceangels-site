Divorce in Michigan:
What It Means for Your Finances

Michigan divides property "equitably," not automatically 50/50, and leaves spousal support to the judge. Before you make decisions about your home, your support, and your retirement, here is how Michigan actually handles the money, in plain language.

Reviewed against Michigan divorce law as of the date noted. General financial guidance, not legal advice.

A Michigan divorce is, at its heart, a financial event. The legal process matters, but most of the decisions that shape your next ten years are about money: who keeps the house, how retirement accounts are split, whether support is paid and for how long. This guide walks through how Michigan handles each of those questions so you can plan with a clear head instead of guessing.

The short version

Michigan divides property by equitable distribution using the Sparks factors, which is fair, not automatically equal. Spousal support has no formula and is left to the judge. Child support runs on the Michigan Child Support Formula, based on both incomes and parenting time.

How Michigan divides property

Michigan is an equitable distribution state, not a community property state. Under MCL 552.19 and 552.401, the court divides marital property in a way that is just and reasonable, applying the nine factors from Sparks v. Sparks: the length of the marriage, each spouse's contributions, age, health, earning ability, the standard of living, needs, conduct, and general equity. The goal is fairness, not mathematical equality, though long marriages often end up close to even. Separate property, owned before the marriage or inherited, generally stays with its owner.

The house and the retirement accounts

The two biggest assets in most divorces are the family home and retirement savings. A retirement account built up during the marriage is marital property even if it is in one spouse's name, and dividing certain accounts takes a separate court order so the transfer is not taxed as an early withdrawal. Debts are divided too. Trading the house for the retirement account is a common move that can quietly cost a great deal if the after-tax values are not compared properly. Spousal support

Michigan has no spousal support formula. Under MCL 552.23, the court weighs a list of factors developed in case law, including the length of the marriage, each spouse's earning ability, age and health, the standard of living, and conduct during the marriage. Michigan is a no-fault state for granting the divorce, but conduct such as wasting marital assets can still affect both property division and support. Awards are often rehabilitative and time-limited.

A note on taxes

For divorce or separation agreements signed after December 31, 2018, spousal support is not deductible for the person paying and not taxable income for the person receiving it, under the federal Tax Cuts and Jobs Act. This changes the real cost and value of every support number, so pre-tax and after-tax figures should never be confused at the negotiating table.

Child support

Michigan calculates child support with the Michigan Child Support Formula, based on both parents' incomes and the number of overnights each parent has with the children, plus add-ons such as childcare and health care. Because it is formula-driven, child support is more predictable than spousal support.

The financial timeline

Two things people consistently underestimate: how long the process takes and how much costs rise once you are running a household alone. A realistic financial sequence:

  • Before filing — gather a complete picture of assets, debts, income, and expenses. Every later decision rests on these.
  • Temporary orders — temporary support and living arrangements while the case proceeds.
  • Disclosure and negotiation — both sides exchange financial information; settlement scenarios get modeled and compared.
  • Settlement or trial — most Michigan divorces settle. The ones that go to trial cost more and hand the decisions to a judge.
  • After the judgment — the distribution, support, and any account transfers are carried out.

Questions worth asking before you negotiate

  • What is the full marital estate, and which assets are actually separate property?
  • If we trade the house for the retirement account, are we comparing after-tax values?
  • Given our incomes and the length of the marriage, what is the realistic range for support?
  • What will my true monthly cost of living be once I am on my own?
  • What does each settlement scenario look like five and ten years out, not just on signing day?

Michigan divorce: common questions

Is Michigan a community property state?

No. Michigan is an equitable distribution state. Under MCL 552.19 and 552.401 the court divides marital property fairly using the nine Sparks v. Sparks factors, which can mean an unequal split, though long marriages often land near 50/50. Separate property, owned before the marriage or inherited, generally stays with its owner.

How is spousal support decided in Michigan?

Michigan has no formula. Under MCL 552.23 the court weighs factors drawn from case law, including the length of the marriage, each spouse's earning ability, age and health, the standard of living, and conduct during the marriage. Awards are often rehabilitative and time-limited.

Does fault affect a Michigan divorce?

Michigan is a no-fault state for granting the divorce, but conduct can still affect property division and spousal support. The Sparks factors include the parties' past relations and conduct, though courts cannot let fault dominate the entire division.

How is child support calculated in Michigan?

Michigan uses the Michigan Child Support Formula, based on both parents' incomes and the number of overnights each parent has with the children, plus add-ons such as childcare and health care.

Is spousal support taxable in Michigan?

For agreements signed after December 31, 2018, spousal support is not deductible by the paying spouse and not taxable income to the receiving spouse, under federal law.
