Divorce in Arizona:
What It Means for Your Finances

Arizona is a community property state, and as of 2022 it calculates spousal maintenance with new guidelines aimed at getting the lower earner self-sufficient. Before you make decisions about your home, your support, and your retirement, here is how Arizona actually handles the money, in plain language.

Reviewed against the Arizona Revised Statutes and the Arizona Spousal Maintenance Guidelines. General financial guidance, not legal advice.

An Arizona divorce is, at its heart, a financial event. The legal process matters, but most of the decisions that shape your next ten years are about money: who keeps the house, how retirement accounts are split, whether support is paid and for how long. This guide walks through how Arizona handles each of those questions so you can plan with a clear head instead of guessing.

The short version

Arizona is a community property state, dividing community property equitably (usually close to equal) while each spouse keeps their separate property. The big recent change: since September 2022, spousal maintenance is set by statutory guidelines aimed at self-sufficiency. Child support runs on the income shares model.

How Arizona divides property

Arizona is a community property state (ARS 25-211): property acquired during the marriage is owned by both spouses. On divorce, ARS 25-318 directs the court to assign each spouse's sole and separate property to that spouse and to divide the community property equitably, though not necessarily in kind, without regard to marital misconduct. In practice, "equitably" usually means a roughly equal split absent a specific reason to deviate. Separate property means assets owned before the marriage or received by gift or inheritance.

The house and the retirement accounts

The two biggest assets in most divorces are the family home and retirement savings. Retirement built up during the relationship counts even if the account is in one person's name, and dividing certain accounts takes a separate order so the transfer is not taxed as an early withdrawal. Debts count too. Trading the house for the retirement savings is a common move that can quietly cost a great deal if the after-tax values are not compared properly. Spousal maintenance

Arizona handles maintenance in two stages. First, the spouse must qualify under ARS 25-319 — for example, lacking enough property or income for reasonable needs, being unable to be self-sufficient, caring for a young child, having made career sacrifices, or a long marriage at an age that limits work. Then comes the part that changed in 2022: for cases filed on or after September 24, 2022, the court must apply the Arizona Spousal Maintenance Guidelines, developed by the state Supreme Court, to set the amount and duration. The award is meant to last only as long as needed for the recipient to become self-sufficient. This replaced a purely discretionary system with a calculation, making outcomes far more predictable.

A note on taxes

For agreements signed after December 31, 2018, spousal support is not deductible for the person paying and not taxable income for the person receiving it, under federal law.

Child support

Arizona uses an income shares model under ARS 25-320 and the Arizona Child Support Guidelines, based on both parents' incomes and parenting time, with add-ons such as health insurance and childcare.

The financial timeline

Two things people consistently underestimate: how long the process takes and how much costs rise once you are running a household alone. A realistic financial sequence: gather a complete financial picture before filing; temporary orders for support while the case proceeds; financial disclosure and negotiation, with settlement scenarios modeled and compared; settlement or trial (most cases settle); and finally carrying out the division, support, and any account transfers after the decree.

Arizona divorce: common questions

Is Arizona a community property state?

Yes. Under ARS 25-211 property acquired during the marriage is community property. On divorce, ARS 25-318 directs the court to assign each spouse's sole and separate property to that spouse and to divide the community property equitably, which in practice usually means close to equal, without regard to marital misconduct.

Can I get spousal maintenance in Arizona?

First you must qualify under ARS 25-319: for example, you lack enough property or income for your reasonable needs, cannot be self-sufficient through work, are caring for a young child, made career sacrifices for the marriage, or had a long marriage at an age that limits employment.

How is the amount of spousal maintenance decided in Arizona?

This changed in 2022. For cases filed on or after September 24, 2022, once a spouse qualifies the court must apply the Arizona Spousal Maintenance Guidelines, set by the state Supreme Court, to calculate the amount and duration. The award is aimed at enabling the recipient to become self-sufficient, which brought far more predictability than the old purely discretionary approach.

How is child support calculated in Arizona?

Arizona uses an income shares model under ARS 25-320 and the Arizona Child Support Guidelines, based on both parents' incomes and parenting time, with add-ons such as health insurance and childcare.

Is spousal maintenance taxable in Arizona?

For agreements signed after December 31, 2018, it is not deductible by the payer and not taxable to the recipient under federal law.
