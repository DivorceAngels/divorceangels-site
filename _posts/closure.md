<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Why does closure seem so important when you are ending your marriage?</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As we let go of our marriage we are in desperate need of the appropriate amount of closure.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>We seek reasons, rationale, and are in search of the perfect explanation for leaving the relationship… hoping for a clean and simple exit.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But closure almost always never happens in the way we want.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And even if we acquire that clarification it will never be enough.&nbsp;&nbsp;There will always be more questions, more angst about how we could have, should have done things differently…</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>More heartache on how it could have played out…</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For those of us struggling for the best answer and evidence to help us heal, it’s best to cut those losses and assume there is going to be none.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>You can’t sum up a complex relationship into a line or two …&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And if you can’t handle the lack of explanation then make up a reason – create a tale that makes you feel ok – because you likely won’t get what you need from your ex.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>The only way you are going to find your closure is when you accept that your&nbsp;relationship&nbsp;is over and you feel a sense of resolution. ... It's an important part of getting over an ex and the breakup so you can feel confident starting a new&nbsp;<strong>relationship</strong>&nbsp;down the road.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Jacob, 43, Texas</p>
