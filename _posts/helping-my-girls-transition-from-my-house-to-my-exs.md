<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>One of the hardest things I experienced in my divorce was dealing with my daughters and my emotions, as they transitioned living from one parents house to another.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>My ex was adamant about sharing our children equally so we agreed that the girls&nbsp;&nbsp;would live with me for one week and with him for the other.&nbsp;&nbsp;We both had homes with bedrooms set up for the girls.&nbsp;&nbsp;They had two sets of clothing and everything they needed at both houses and really only needed to bring their school books and computers from house to house.&nbsp;&nbsp;This all worked well on paper when we first agreed to it in our separation agreement, until my ex had his new girlfriend move in with him.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Every week that it was their turn to go live with their dad, they would experience a host of emotions, including resistance, reluctance and sadness about leaving me for a whole week.&nbsp;&nbsp;The transition was extremely difficult for them (and me)&nbsp;&nbsp;and I knew that they didn’t like spending time with his new girlfriend and were sad and concerned about leaving me alone for the week.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>As hard as it was for all to deal with this transition, I made the decision to not show my own emotions, and to support the children’s transitions between our two homes. I worked to remain conscious of my own feelings, to make sure they didn’t pick up on any of my own sadness and missing them while they were away.&nbsp;&nbsp;I always made sure to reassure them and let them know what I would be doing in my own life while they were at their dads, to make sure they knew I would be okay and to set them at ease.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>At their age (My girls are 9 and 11 years old)&nbsp;&nbsp;spending regular time with both parents is important for their development (even if they didn’t like his new partner).&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The truth is that It is never easy to manage things perfectly, especially with divorce and children.&nbsp;&nbsp;Just when you think you have an understanding and a handle on navigating your divorce,&nbsp;&nbsp;an issue arises that throws everything off course.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In this situation, the most important thing I could do for my girls was to be a good role model, no matter how difficult the situation was, and put their feelings first when it came to dealing with separation, divorce and their Dad.&nbsp;</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>&nbsp;I am hoping that one day, this won’t feel so big for all of us, and that insisting that they stay at their Dad’s every other week will keep their father/daughter relationship&nbsp;&nbsp;strong and remain an integral part of both their lives.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Joanne, 43</p>
