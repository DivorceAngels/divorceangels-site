<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>When I stood at the edge of my marriage, watching everything I knew unravel, I never imagined that the other side would be filled with strength, clarity, and yes — even joy. Divorce is not just the end of a relationship; it’s the end of a routine, a rhythm, and often an identity. But it’s also the beginning of something new.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Let me tell you how I came through my divorce — not just surviving, but truly <em>thriving</em>.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Grief Was Real — But So Was the Growth</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>In the early days, I couldn’t breathe without feeling the weight of loss. I missed the companionship, the shared plans, the illusion of stability. I was terrified. At 50-something, the idea of starting over felt daunting. I grieved, I cried, I questioned everything.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>But slowly, I began to see that grief was a doorway — not a destination. With every day that passed, I began rebuilding the parts of myself I had abandoned during my marriage. I started small: walks by myself, reading books I loved, eating dinner on my terms.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Reclaiming My Identity</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Divorce forced me to ask the question: <em>Who am I now?</em> Not as a wife, not in relation to someone else, but just me.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>I rediscovered passions I had shelved — painting, yoga, gardening. I took a solo trip. I spent time with women who lifted me up. I stopped apologizing for taking up space. Slowly, I remembered that I am whole on my own. I always was.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Financial Empowerment Was Part of My Healing</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Like many women, I was worried about money. But learning to manage my finances became a surprising source of empowerment. I got help. I educated myself. I created a budget, and for the first time in years, I felt in control. That sense of security — even if it came in small steps — gave me peace.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">The Unexpected Gifts</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>There were things I never expected. The peace of not walking on eggshells. The ability to make decisions without compromise. The quiet confidence that comes from knowing I can handle anything. I even found laughter again — real, from-the-gut laughter — the kind I hadn’t felt in years.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">For Anyone on This Path: You’re Not Alone</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>If you're going through a divorce right now, I see you. The late nights, the self-doubt, the wondering if you'll ever feel "normal" again — I’ve lived it. But I promise you this: you <em>will</em> come through. And you may just find a version of yourself that’s wiser, braver, and more radiant than ever before.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Divorce was not the end of my story. It was the bold, beautiful beginning of a life I never imagined — one where I lead with courage, compassion, and fierce self-love.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>And you can too.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
